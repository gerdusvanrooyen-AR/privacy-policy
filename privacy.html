Privacy Policy — Construction Calculator

Effective date: 7 September 2026 · Last updated: 7 September 2026

Construction Calculator (“the app”) is published by NEXERA-IN. This policy explains what the app does and does not do with your information.

The short version. The app does not have accounts and does not require you to sign in. Everything you type stays on your own phone unless you choose to send it. The one thing the app itself sends over the internet is a problem report or a suggestion, and only when you press send on the form that shows you exactly what it contains.

What the app stores on your device

The app saves the following on your phone only, so your work is still there when you reopen it:

  • the figures you type into the calculators, and the results worked out from them
  • your calculation history, including the material list each calculation produced
  • your settings — language, measurement system, and similar preferences
  • the material quantities you enter as available yard stock
  • a problem report or suggestion you sent while the phone had no connection, kept on the phone only until it has been sent
  • the name and contact details you type into a problem report, if you type any, so that you do not have to type them again next time

This data is held in your phone’s own app storage. It is not uploaded, backed up to us, or shared with anyone. Uninstalling the app deletes it. Clearing the app’s data from your phone’s settings deletes it. Inside the app, History has its own Clear, and the yard stock sheet has one too.

Camera, photos and files

Some calculators let you attach a picture or a file — a photo you take, an image already on your phone, or a PDF — as a visual reference while you type. The roof outline screen is the only place this happens.

The app asks your permission before using the camera or your photo library.

No measurement is taken from the picture. Every figure comes from the tape readings you type; a drawing has no scale the phone can read. The picture is there so you can see which side is which while you work.

Pictures and files are handled entirely on your device. When you choose an existing file, your phone copies it into the app’s own temporary storage so it can be displayed, and a PDF’s first page is converted to an image there as well. Those copies live in the app’s cache on your phone and go when the app’s data does. Nothing is uploaded to us and nothing is sent to any server.

When information leaves your phone — only when you send it

There are four places where you, not the app, can send information out. Each one needs a deliberate tap. Two of them hand the information to another app that you choose. The other two send it to the developer over the internet, and the next section says exactly where it goes and what happens to it.

  1. Sharing a result. When you share or export a calculation as a PDF, your phone’s own share menu opens and you choose where it goes — email, WhatsApp, a printer, or a file. The app builds the document on the phone and hands it to your phone’s share menu; it does not transmit it itself, and it does not keep a copy.
  2. Reporting a problem. When you press send, the app sends the report to the developer over the internet. It includes what you typed in the report, the app version, your device platform, which calculator was open, your measurement system, your language, and the figures on screen at the time — this is what makes a fault possible to fix. The app shows you all of it on the form before you send, and nothing is sent unless you press send. If there is no connection, the report is saved on your phone and sent the next time you open the app with a signal; the screen tells you that is what has happened rather than telling you it was sent.
  3. Suggesting a calculator. The same, but carrying only what you typed: the calculator you would like, what it should work out, and your name if you choose to give one. No figures and no calculator are sent with it, because a suggestion is about a calculator that does not exist yet.
    Reports and suggestions are received by the developer and are used only to fix faults and decide what to build next.
  4. Searching the web. Where a calculator offers a web search, tapping it opens a Google search in your browser with the words you chose. From that point Google’s own privacy policy applies, not this one. Nothing the browser returns comes back into a calculation.

Where a report goes

A report or a suggestion goes to a private inbox belonging to the developer, inside the developer’s own Google account: it is written to a Google Sheet that only the developer can read, and a copy is emailed to the developer. Google holds it as the developer’s service provider and does not receive it for its own purposes. It is not sent anywhere else and no other company receives it.

Nothing identifying your phone or you is sent with it: no device identifier, no advertising identifier, no installation identifier, no location and no account. Your name and your contact details are sent only if you type them into the form, and both are optional — a report without them is still a complete report.

It is used only to fix the fault you reported, or to decide what to build next. It is never sold, never shared with anybody else, never used for advertising, and never used to build a picture of you.

Reports are kept while they are being dealt with and for a reasonable period afterwards, so that the same fault reported twice can be recognised as the same fault.

To have a report deleted, send a new report from the app asking for it and saying roughly when you sent the first one, or write to gerdusvanrooyen@gmail.com. It will be deleted.

What the app does not do

  • It does not collect analytics, usage tracking, or advertising identifiers.
  • It does not show adverts.
  • It does not sell or share personal information.
  • It does not use your location.
  • It does not require your name, address, phone number or identity number. The problem report form offers a name and contact field so we can reply to you; both are optional and both are used only for that.
  • It does not contact any server on its own. The only thing that ever leaves your phone over the internet is a report or a suggestion that you have typed and sent, as described above.

Permissions the app declares

  • Camera — real, and used in one place: photographing a roof drawing, as described above.
  • Internet — used for one thing: sending a problem report or a suggestion when you press send. Nothing else in the app uses the network.
  • Storage (Android 12 and older) and Vibrate — declared by the components the app is built from, and not used by the app’s own code.

Children

The app is a work tool for the building trade and is not directed at children.

Your rights

Because the data is on your own device, you control it: you can clear your history in the app, clear your yard stock in the app, clear the app’s data in your phone’s settings, or uninstall the app to remove all of it. To have a report you have already sent deleted, see Where a report goes above.

This policy is written to meet South Africa’s Protection of Personal Information Act (POPIA) and the equivalent expectations of the Google Play and Apple App Store listings.

Changes to this policy

If the app starts doing something this policy does not describe, the policy is updated before that version is published, and the date at the top changes.
